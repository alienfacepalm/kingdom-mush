Hold the road. An original three-march tower defense, drawn from how Kingdom Rush puts a fight on screen.

This page illustrates the design spec. Names, figures, and roads are original. No Ironhide art or level geometry is copied.

What the screens are doing

Two public Kingdom Rush screens set the layout. The middle of the road stays empty. Information sits on the edges, and the next decision sits on the pad or the entrance.

1Lives, gold, and wave sit top left. Pause and 2× sit top right.
2Build sites are fixed pads on the grass. A click opens Bow, Den, Hex, and Bomb on the pad.
3The exit is a flagged hold. A foe that arrives costs one life.
4The wave control sits by the entrance. One click previews. Call sends the march. Early gold is the seconds still on the clock.
5Aid and Spore Rain live bottom left, locked until their wave.
6The bottom bar names the selected pad, tower, or foe. Grey gold means the tower is too expensive.

Player sequence

One march is a loop of spending, calling, and answering what the road sends. The clock between waves is money if you call early, and a breather if you wait.

One simulation tick

The fight steps at 30 Hz. Drawing never decides a hit. Each tick runs in this order so blockers, movement, and shots agree.

Damage flow

A hit is a roll, then a protection, then a floor. Armor, magic resistance, and true damage are different doors into the same formula: floor(amount × (1 − protection)).

Bow hits air Hex misses air, ignores armor Bomb aims at the ground; the blast still clips fliers Toughest shell is 62% armor, so a physical hit still lands

Four lines, then a branch

Every pad grows the same way. Two upgrades, then a specialty that includes its trick. Dewcap stops at tier 3 so the first march teaches upgrading before the choice. There is no second shop of paid abilities on top of the branch.

Sell and rally

Before wave 1, selling returns the full cost. After that, 60%. Rally points sit on the road and within reach of the barracks. Upgrading a barracks refreshes the squad.

Studies, one star each

Eagle Caps (range), Thick Caps (barracks health), Arcane Study (mage damage), Wide Puffs (blast radius), Blitz Spores (early gold), Veterans (reinforcements). Reset refunds the stars.

Warden Puff

Absent on Dewcap. From Forkfen, right-click sends the warden. They hold one ground foe once they arrive, and H fires a short true-damage burst. Aid is two soldiers for a short stand. Spore Rain is three true hits in a circle.

How a barracks holds the road

Who gets the front

  1. Foes are ordered by how far they have walked. The one closest to the hold is claimed first.
  2. A soldier already in range takes that foe. Extras pile on.
  3. The foe hits only its holder.
  4. When the holder dies, the next free soldier picks the foe up.
  5. Flying foes skip the hold and are answered by archers, thrown axes, musket splash, or chain lightning.

Three marches

Southport is the template for the first road: a short path, about 250 gold, 20 lives, sproutlings, then shells, reinforcements partway through, and a true-damage rain near the end. The later roads teach a split, then a boss.

What the review kept

Stays, because this is the game

  • Fixed pads and four tower lines
  • A tier-4 branch whose trick is included
  • Blocking, armor versus magic, fliers
  • One hero, two powers, early wave calls
  • Stars that buy six studies
  • A labeled Call button and a 2× speed

Cut, so a playable game could ship

  • A full campaign and heroic or iron modes
  • A hero roster and a gem shop
  • A separate shop of three paid abilities per tower
  • Immune armor. The shell tops out at 62%

The engineering cut is the same idea: the simulation does not import the renderer. Balance tests drive it with a fixed seed and a scripted commander that is not shipped to the player. Boss adds are capped so a fight cannot run forever.
